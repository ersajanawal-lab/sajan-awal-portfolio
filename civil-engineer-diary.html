My journey from civil engineer to project manager

My experience includes residential drawings and estimates, construction inspections, site engineering, and construction project management. This Diary is where I connect that experience with the questions and lessons behind the work.

  1. Civil engineering foundations

    Residential drawings, site measurement, estimates, valuations, and technical documentation formed part of my early engineering responsibilities.

  2. Site engineering at Dharmashala

    My work included setting out, site supervision, bar bending schedules, estimates, payment information, and progress reporting.

  3. Project delivery at Dharahara

    My responsibilities included planning, coordination, supervision, procurement support, and progress and payment documentation.

  4. Continuing to learn

    I am completing a Master of Construction Management at CQUniversity in Sydney and developing my understanding of Australian construction practice.

A story I’m developing

In development — not a completed case study

Developing Formwork for a Spherical RCC Shear Wall with Ramp and Beam Intersections

At Dharahara, I was involved in developing formwork for a reinforced-concrete shear wall curved in both horizontal and vertical directions, where a ramp and beam intersected the wall.

I am documenting my exact contribution, the arrangement developed, the review process, and the available evidence before publishing the full case study.

The planned story will distinguish my responsibilities from the team’s work and separate confirmed challenges from assumptions. It will not claim unverified outcomes or provide a reusable formwork design.

From experience to practical learning

Each developed story will work through five questions:

  1. What was happening on the project?
  2. What information, understanding, or coordination gap needed attention?
  3. What was my responsibility, and what action was taken?
  4. What outcome can I support with evidence?
  5. What should another professional clarify before acting?

A learning boundary

These stories support professional learning. They do not replace approved drawings, project specifications, site procedures, project-specific design and review, or advice from appropriately qualified professionals.
